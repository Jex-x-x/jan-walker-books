The Supra exists because an engine would not fit. When Toyota decided in the late 1970s that it wanted an inline-six in the Celica, the engine was longer than the bay it had to live in, so the car was stretched by about five inches ahead of the windscreen and given a longer bonnet to cover it. That is the whole origin: a Celica with more nose. Toyota named the result with the Latin word for above — supra — and in Japan badged it Celica XX, pro‐ nounced Double X, which is why a Japanese enthusiast and an American one can describe the same car for several minutes before realising they are talking about the same thing.
