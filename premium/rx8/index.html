Inside the book

10 parts. 90 questions.

A sports car with four doors, no pillar between them and an engine with no pistons. It sounds like a committee’s compromise. It was approved on one condition from management, and it won an International Engine of the Year award in its first year. The story starts well before the car. A German engineer designed a rotary compressor in the 1920s. A motorcycle maker turned it into an engine, a small Japanese company bet its future on it, and in 1991 one of its racing cars won the 24 Hours of Le Mans, which no car without pistons has done since.
