Pajero Sport Trivia & Fun Facts

Ninety questions, and for each of them the source that settles it. Mitsubishi's own announcements supply the launch dates, plant openings, engine and transmission firsts and the plans for the new Pajero. Crash-test results come from ANCAP and Latin NCAP directly. Capability figures — towing, fording, angles — come from Mitsubishi Australia's published specifications and are marked as Australian. Sales and discontinuation decisions come from the trade press of the market concerned.

Wikipedia was used to find primary sources and is never the only source for an answer; owner forums tell you which question to ask, never the answer. Every one of the ninety answers rests on at least two sources. Five of the boxes rest on one — mostly the maker describing its own factory or its own equipment — and each is marked one source. Where two respectable sources disagree, the book says so in the answer rather than choosing the more impressive number.

Corrections made while checking

Found by checking every claim one at a time and in an independent review before publication; the last three were found after publication, on the reference pages at the back of the book, and corrected in the files on sale. Listed here rather than quietly applied.

  • End of Japanese sales. Was: 2003, as in the English-language encyclopedia → now: 2001. Japanese motoring publications agree on June 2001; the disagreement is stated in the answer.
  • Jutta Kleinschmidt. Was: “an engineer by training who moved to cars in the 1990s” → now: studied physics, worked at BMW, first rode the rally on a motorcycle in 1988. The original wording could not be sourced; verifiable facts replaced it.
  • The rally prototypes. Was: “by the late 1990s” → now: from 2002, after a rule change. Full prototypes were allowed from 2002; the earlier date was wrong.
  • The Nativa name. Was: “Latin America and the Middle East” → now: Puerto Rico and parts of Latin America. The Middle East rested on a single source; Puerto Rico is confirmed by a US emissions listing and Colombia by local press.
  • Sales in 2003. Was: a question on Montero Sport units sold, single source → now: the Endeavor target of 80,000 a year. The 20,306 figure appears in one trade source only; the 80,000 target is confirmed twice. The sales figure stays in the explanation with attribution.
  • The petrol V6. Was: “the Gulf states and Mexico”, “Vietnam at launch” → now: Mexico and Chile, with output from Mitsubishi Chile. The Gulf and Vietnam rested on one encyclopedia; the Chilean brochure gives 207 hp and 279 Nm.
  • The Philippines. Was: “kept the Montero name the full-size model had carried there” → now: used the Montero Sport name from the first generation. The full-size vehicle was sold as the Pajero in the Philippines, so the original claim was wrong.
  • Third Laem Chabang plant. Was: 2012 → now: 2007, per Mitsubishi Motors Thailand. A trade report and the company release disagree; the company date is used.
  • The Challenger ANCAP rating. Was: no context → now: based on a 2006 frontal offset test of the Triton pickup. ANCAP states this on its own page: the SUV was not crashed separately.
  • The tail lamps. Was: “from the top of the tailgate almost to the bumper” → now: “large vertical lamps cascading down the rear corners”, “cut down in size”. No source described the geometry that precisely.
  • Spoilers. Was: three questions were answerable from earlier text → now: rewritten or moved. Found by the spoiler checker and a manual pass over relative time references.
  • The spread of correct answers. Was: A16 / B32 / C26 / D16 → now: A23 / B23 / C22 / D22, no letter more than twice in a row. Shuffled with the series script; 38 ascending numeric lists set by hand.
  • Rear differential lock. Was: “higher grades; the entry-level Australian version went without” → now: GLS and Exceed, the whole 2025 Australian range. The 2025 brochure lists no entry-level version without it; question and explanation were rewritten.
  • Pragoti Industries. Was: the ministry, the Pajero since 1995, a plan of 500 a year → now: only the Mitsubishi link since 1977. Three details rested on a search summary with no primary text; only what Automotive World states was kept.
  • The world-first diesel. Was: stated as fact → now: attributed to Mitsubishi. The world-first claim is Mitsubishi's own; the question now says so.
  • The GDI V6 callout. Was: “the second six-cylinder direct-injection petrol engine ever” → now: five GDI engines, 600,000 built and 75 per cent of Japanese cars by September 1999. The historical ordinal rested on two encyclopedia articles; the new figures come from Fleet News, quoting Mitsubishi.
  • Felis pajeros. Was: “Mitsubishi press material used Felis pajeros” → now: Mitsubishi's company history on why the name was chosen. The older name rested on an encyclopedia paraphrase; Mitsubishi's own history is quoted instead.
  • Method statement. Was: a narrower sourcing rule than was applied → now: describes the sources actually used; every answer has a non-encyclopedia source. The book promised more than the check delivered; two answers gained Mitsubishi primary sources.
  • The names list. Was: “Nativa — parts of Latin America and the Middle East” → now: “Nativa — Puerto Rico and parts of Latin America”. The Middle East was removed from the answer as single-sourced but survived on the reference page. Found after publication, in a pass over the reference pages that the first check had not covered.
  • The timeline, 1997. Was: “The Montero Sport arrives in the United States” → now: “…for the 1997 model year”. The sources give the model year, not a calendar date of arrival.
  • The timeline, disputed dates. Was: 2001 and December 2022 given without qualification → now: both give the other figure the sources report. The method statement names both dates as disputed and the answers say so; the timeline did not.
