Jan Walker Books · sources

Ultimate Toyota Challenge

Ninety questions, and for each of them the source that settles it. Figures and dates come from the company's own filings and from regulators wherever those exist; anything a manufacturer had reason to phrase generously is checked against an independent test. Owner forums are used to find out which question is worth asking, never to answer it.

Two retellings of one press release are one source, not two. Eight of these entries rest on a single source; each is marked one source and says in its note why a second was not found. Where the record is genuinely disputed, the book says so in the answer instead of choosing the more impressive number.

Corrections made before publication

Found by checking every question one at a time, question by question rather than part by part. These claims did not survive that check in their first form. Listed here rather than quietly fixed.

  • The Corolla box. Was: a claim about survey scores for desire and repurchase intent → now: an observation with no statistical claim. The survey claim could not be sourced, so it was removed rather than softened.
  • The pickup in conflicts. Was: a vague statement that it turned up in that role → now: the 1987 Chad border war, known as the Toyota War, named specifically. A vague claim was replaced with a documented one.
  • The position on battery-only cars. Was: stated without a figure → now: the chairman's thirty-per-cent forecast and the multi-pathway wording. The public position is evidenced by a specific statement, so the book quotes it.
  • The Le Mans rule box. Was: “too slowly” without figures → now: the six-minute limit, and the actual 11:53.815. The organisers publish their own explanation with the exact time.
  • The first operating loss. Was: “in seventy years”, in the same period → now: seventy-one years, in the financial year that followed. The loss falls in the year ended March 2009 and was the first in seventy-one years.
  • The price of the patent. Was: GBP 100,000, presented as fully paid in four instalments → now: GBP 100,000 as the contract price, with GBP 83,500 actually paid. The company's own 75-year history records the July 1934 renegotiation: the unpaid GBP 61,500 was cut to GBP 45,000.
  • How many continents. Was: four → now: five. The explanation itself listed five — Asia, North and South America, Europe and Africa. The question now asks for the count.
  • The plural referendum. Was: “lost by one point” → now: won by one point. The box's own figures give Prii twenty-five per cent against twenty-four: the conclusion contradicted the data.
  • The confusion over the F1 code. Was: sixty years → now: four decades. The project began in 1983, which is forty-three years to 2026.
  • The Lexus launch. Was: “a saloon and a coupé”, January 1989 → now: two saloons, the LS 400 and the ES 250; unveiled in January, on sale from 1 September. Both first models were four-door saloons, and the show debut is now distinguished from the start of sales.
  • The age of the company. Was: “ninety years, 1937–2026” → now: the date range alone. 1937 to 2026 is eighty-nine years.
  • Four questions scored on opinion. Was: answers that cannot be checked → now: verifiable facts. Memorability of advertising, which market took a vehicle “most seriously”, the “lasting lesson” of a recall and a universal power ceiling are judgements, and a quiz cannot award points for them. They were replaced with the plus-100-cc campaign, the 70 Series returning to Japan, the seven main bearings and the size of the Department of Justice settlement.
  • The spread of correct answers. Was: A22 / B60 / C8 / D0 → now: A23 / B23 / C22 / D22. A reader who always answered B scored 60 out of 90 and a high rank while knowing nothing about the marque.
